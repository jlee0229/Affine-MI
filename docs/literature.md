## Section 1 — Scoop verdict

### Short answer

**Not scooped on the specific claim.** Nobody has connected *monomiality*, *M-groups*, *induction from a non-trivial linear character*, or a *twisted (character-weighted) coset structure* to neural-network feature organization or to the Chughtai-vs-Stander dispute. The words "monomial" and "M-group" do not occur in Chughtai 2023, Stander 2024, Wu 2024/25, or Morwani 2024, nor in any 2025–2026 follow-up I could find. An arXiv-API sweep for (`"induced representation"` OR `monomial` OR `"Frobenius reciprocity"`) AND (`grokking` OR `"mechanistic interpretability"` OR `"group composition"` OR `"group multiplication"` OR `"Cayley table"`) returns 4 papers, none relevant (a LoRA/SAE paper, a 1994 R-groups paper, a holomorphic-monomial-activation grokking paper, and "On immanants of Cayley tables"). A sweep for (`"affine group"` OR `"Frobenius group"` OR `"semidirect product"` OR `"Ramanujan sum(s)"`) AND (`grokking` OR `"mechanistic interpretability"` OR `"modular addition"` OR `"modular arithmetic"` OR `"group composition"`) returns **zero** papers.

**Partly scooped on the underlying idea — by Wu, Jaburi, Drori, Gross (ICLR 2025) and, less explicitly, by Stander et al. (2024) — but only for the trivial-character case.** Be precise about what they already have:

1. **The "two descriptions are the same object" statement already exists for the trivial character.** Wu et al. define a *projected ρ-set* (a neuron of the form g ↦ aᵀρ(g)b where b has a non-trivial stabilizer H) and prove (their Lemma G.6, PDF numbering) that a function on G is *both* irrep-sparse (Chughtai's condition) *and* coset-concentrated (Stander's condition) **if and only if** it is a projected ρ-set. Their proof literally builds the permutation representation on G/H and says, verbatim: "(That is, ρ̃ is induced by the trivial representation on H.)" (VERIFIED, Appendix G, proof of Lemma G.6.) In the main text: "the map g ↦ ρ(g)b is constant on precisely the cosets of the stabilizer Stab_G(b) := {g ∈ G | ρ(g)b = b}. Further, since the action of G is transitive, Stab_G(b′) for any other element b′ is a conjugate of Stab_G(b); as a consequence left and right preactivations of each neuron concentrate on cosets of conjugate subgroups." (VERIFIED, §6.3.) So the reconciliation "irrep-sparse neurons *are* coset-constant neurons because b is an H-fixed vector" is published.

2. **Frobenius reciprocity is already used in this exact literature — by Stander et al., for the trivial character.** Stander 2024 Appendix G.2, Lemma G.4 and proof (VERIFIED): "By Frobenius reciprocity, ⟨Ind_H^G(1_H), χ(ρ)⟩ = ⟨1_H, χ(Res_H(ρ))⟩_H … The left-hand side ⟨Ind_H^G(1_H), χ(ρ)⟩ is nonzero if and only if ρ is an irreducible component of the permutation representation of G on G/H. The right-hand side ⟨1_H, χ(Res_H(ρ))⟩_H is nonzero if and only if dim(V^H) ≠ 0." This is exactly the trivial-character instance of the reciprocity argument your hypothesis generalizes. Stander's Table 5 (VERIFIED, Appendix, p. 27) then tabulates, for every conjugacy class of subgroups of S5, the fraction of the Fourier energy of the centered indicator 1_H landing on each irrep — e.g. S4 → 100 % on the standard irrep, A5 → 100 % on sign, F20 → 100 % on one 5-dim irrep, A4 → 44.4 %/44.4 %/11.2 % split across three irreps.

3. **The 6-dimensional irrep of S5 is already placed in this framework — but with a different subgroup than yours.** Wu et al. Appendix H, Table 2 (VERIFIED) lists, for every S5 irrep, the *minimum ρ-set size and its stabilizer*: trivial 1 (S5); sign 2 (A5); standard 5 (S4); sign-standard 10 (A4); 5d-0 6 (F5 = F20); 5d-1 12 (D12); **6d-0 20 (Z/6Z or S3)**. Table 3 lists every transitive permutation representation of S5 of size ≤ 30 with its irrep decomposition, and includes "Z/5Z 24 1d-0, 1d-1, 5d-0, 5d-1, 6d-0" — i.e. they already know the 6d irrep has a C5-fixed vector (a ρ-set of size 24 with stabilizer C5), though they never discuss it beyond the table. They never mention F20 in connection with the 6d irrep, never mention a non-trivial character, and never mention any "twist."

4. **What Wu et al. explicitly say is missing from the trivial-character picture is precisely the door you are walking through.** VERIFIED, §6.3: "This lemma [Stander's] fails to fully explain irrep sparsity, since the permutation representation of G on G/H is never itself an irrep; it always contains the trivial irrep, possibly among others. Thus, it does not explain why the models' neurons are supported purely on single irreps and not, say, on linear combinations of each irrep with the trivial irrep." [INFERENCE] Induction from a *non-trivial* linear character is exactly the construction that yields a representation that *can* be irreducible (monomial irreps) without a trivial summand. Nobody has pointed this out in the ML literature.

### Two warnings about the hypothesis itself (my analysis, please check)

**Warning A — the "non-monomial irreps should not show coset organization" prediction conflicts with the published data.** [INFERENCE, but built on VERIFIED facts.] Stander's headline S5 circuit is the "conjugate subgroup circuit": neurons constant on cosets of H_i ≅ S4 (point stabilizers) — and Stander's own Lemma G.6 + Table 5 show that these S4-coset neurons live in the **standard 4-dimensional irrep**, which is *not* monomial (S5 has no subgroup of index 4). Wu et al.'s Table 2 gives a non-trivial stabilizer (hence a coset-constant basis vector) for **every** irrep of S5, including 4, 4′, 5, 5′ (stabilizers S4, A4, F20, D12). So "coset organization" in the Stander/Wu sense (neuron = aᵀρ(g)b with b fixed by some H ≠ 1) is available for *all* S5 irreps and is empirically observed for the non-monomial standard irrep. If your dichotomy is to survive it must be sharpened to something the trivial-character ρ-set picture does *not* give — e.g. (i) the *full* induced basis {ρ(g_i)v} with v a χ-eigenvector, i.e. a family of |G/H| neurons indexed by G/H all of the same shape, vs. Wu's single H-fixed vector; or (ii) a twist by a non-trivial χ; or (iii) an explicit statement that on M-groups *every* irrep-sparse neuron can be written as a twisted coset indicator, whereas on S5 the 4d neurons cannot be.

**Warning B — the F20/C5 twist is implied by restriction alone, so it does not by itself discriminate "monomial induced basis" from "Wu ρ-set with stabilizer C5".** [INFERENCE — hand computation from the S5 character table, please re-derive.] Ind_{F20}^{S5} χ for χ a character of order 4 has character (6, 0, −2, 0, 1, 0, 0) on classes (e, (12), (12)(34), (123), (12345), (1234), (123)(45)) = the 6d irrep, so 6 is monomial as you say; Ind_{F20} 1 = 1 + 5′ and Ind_{F20} χ² = sgn + 5. The C5-fixed subspace of the 6d irrep is 2-dimensional (consistent with Wu Table 3: 6d-0 appears in the size-24 permutation rep with stabilizer Z/5Z with multiplicity 2) and, as a representation of F20/C5 ≅ C4, has character (2, 0, −2, 0) = χ + χ̄. Hence **every** C5-invariant vector in the 6d irrep is rotated by 90° under a generator of F20/C5. So a neuron g ↦ aᵀρ₆(g)b with b C5-fixed is automatically "constant on C5-cosets with a 4-periodic rotation across F20/C5" — the twist is a property of Res_{F20} ρ₆, not an extra signature of the monomial construction. A sharper discriminating prediction would be about *which* orbit the network picks: Wu's minimal ρ-set for the 6d irrep is the 20-element orbit with stabilizer S3 (or C6), not the 24-element C5 orbit; a monomial/induced-basis account predicts the 6 F20-cosets × 4 characters structure. Also note Chughtai found the 6d irrep is **never** learned by ReLU MLPs on S5 (Section 4), so the prediction is only testable if you can force or encourage it (Morwani's quadratic-activation networks do use it, Section 4).

## Section 2 — Wu, Jaburi, Drori, Gross (2024/ICLR 2025) in detail

**Title:** "Towards a Unified and Verified Understanding of Group-Operation Networks" (arXiv v1 title: "Unifying and Verifying Mechanistic Interpretations: A Case Study with Group Operations"). arXiv:2410.07476, ICLR 2025 spotlight. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07476 . Note: section numbers below follow the ICLR/PDF version (arXiv HTML v2 numbers are one higher, e.g. PDF §6.3 = HTML §7.1).

**Setup.** One-hidden-layer ReLU MLP, left/right embeddings w_l, w_r, unembedding w_u; 100 models on S5 (m = 128, 40 % train split, Adam wd 2e-4, 25k epochs); also S4 and A5 (App. K.1); Z/53Z used only as a control for interventions (App. E).

**Neuron form (Observation B.1, from Chughtai):** w_l^i(x) ≈ tr(ρ(x)A_i), w_r^i(x) ≈ tr(ρ(x)B_i), w_u^i(x) ≈ tr(ρ(x)C_i). **New observations (B.2):** for real irreps A_i, B_i, C_i are rank one; C_i ≈ r_i B_i A_i; A_i = a_i d_iᵀ etc. with a_i approximately constant across all neurons on the same irrep (a shared vector a_ρ); the b_i vectors of the neurons on ρ partition into finite sets B_{ρ,q} on which "ρ acts on each partition by permutations; that is, ρ induces a left G-set structure on every B_{ρ,q}. We say that such B_{ρ,q} is a ρ-set" (VERIFIED, Obs. B.2(6)).

**Idealized circuit (Eq. 3/4, VERIFIED):** f_{ρ,B}(z|x,y) = −Σ_{b,b′∈B} bᵀρ(z)b′ · ReLU[bᵀρ(x)a − aᵀρ(y)b′], which after the change of variables depends only on x⁻¹zy⁻¹ (bi-equivariance, Prop. B.3: "f_θ(z|g₁x, yg₂) = f_θ(g₁⁻¹zg₂⁻¹|x,y)"), and is maximized at z = xy. **Sign irrep (§5.2, VERIFIED):** "for the sign irrep, the model is able to use one-dimensionality to avoid the expense of the double summation in Eq 4. Explicitly, up to scaling, the sign circuit is f_sgn(z|x,y) = ρ(z) − ρ(z)ReLU[ρ(x) − ρ(y)] − ρ(z)ReLU[ρ(y) − ρ(x)] = ρ(x⁻¹zy⁻¹)". Footnote: "any real-valued one-dimensional irrep of any group must take values ±1, and the same circuit as described here works."

**The reconciliation (§6.3, all VERIFIED):**
- Def. 6.1 (Chughtai): irrep sparse ⇔ f(g) = tr(ρ(g)A). Def. 6.2 (Stander): coset concentrated ⇔ constant on the cosets of some non-trivial H. Def. 6.3: "A function f : G → R is a projected ρ-set for ρ ∈ Irrep(G) of dimension d if there exist a, b ∈ R^d such that f(g) = aᵀρ(g)b and b is in a ρ-set with nontrivial stabilizer."
- "Projected ρ-sets are irrep-sparse. This fact, while immediate from definitions, resolves a mystery from Chughtai et al. (2023, Figure 7): why is the standard irrep ρ_std of S5 learned significantly more frequently than the sign-standard irrep ρ_sgnstd, when both have the same dimensionality …? The answer is that the smallest ρ_std-set has size 5, while the smallest ρ_sgnstd-set has size 10 (Appendix H). Thus a minimum complete ρ_std-set circuit needs 5² = 25 neurons, while a minimum complete ρ_sgnstd-set circuit needs 10² = 100. The order of frequencies with which ρ ∈ Irrep(G) is learned … is the same as the ordering of Irrep(G) by minimum ρ-set size (Table 2), not by dimensionality."
- "Projected ρ-sets are coset concentrated": stabilizer argument quoted in Section 1.
- "Coset concentration fails to explain irrep sparsity": paraphrases Stander's lemma (their Lemma 6.4) and objects that G/H "is never itself an irrep; it always contains the trivial irrep." Also: "since there are many more subgroups than irreps (Stander et al., 2024, Appendix G.3), most subgroups H ≤ G have corresponding actions of G on G/H that decompose into more than two irreps."
- "Projected ρ-sets are equivalent to the conjunction of irrep sparsity and coset concentration. Note that neither irrep sparsity nor coset concentration alone is equivalent … [counterexample: the character χ(g) = tr ρ(g) is irrep-sparse but not coset-concentrated] … if f : G → R is both irrep-sparse and coset-concentrated, then it must be a projected ρ-set; for a proof see Lemma G.6. Hence, if we consider by itself a single embedding of a single neuron on f_θ, then our Observation B.2(2) is logically equivalent to the combination of observations from previous works."
- Lemma G.6 (VERIFIED): "Suppose f : G → C is coset concentrated and irrep sparse … Then, there must exist an embedding ι : G/H → C^d such that the action of ρ on ι(G/H) is isomorphic to the action of G on G/H. Further, there exist a ∈ (C^d)* and b ∈ C^d such that f(g) = ⟨a, ρ(g)b⟩. The converse also holds and is immediate." Proof constructs ρ̃ = permutation rep on G/H "(That is, ρ̃ is induced by the trivial representation on H.)" and projects e_{g₁} to the ρ-isotypic component.

**Terms searched in the PDF text:** "induced" — 1 substantive hit (the parenthetical above); "monomial", "M-group", "linear character", "one-dimensional character", "twist" — 0 hits; "Frobenius" — only Frobenius-Schur indicator (App. K.2) and Frobenius norm/inner product; "stabilizer" — 9 hits, all in the trivial-character sense.

**Which irreps / 6d (App. H, VERIFIED):** Table 2 and Table 3 as quoted in Section 1. "Notice that the ordering of Irrep(S5) by minimum ρ-set size matches the ordering by frequencies with which irreps are learned (Chughtai et al., 2023, Figure 7)." Main-text experiments only analyse the standard and sign circuits; "we only claim to understand roughly half of the models we examine, all of which are trained on S5" (§7). Failure modes "(a-bad)" and "(ρ-bad)" — the latter is missing pairs in the double sum.

**Beyond S5 (App. K, VERIFIED):** K.1 — S_n is nice because all irreps are real and "the minimum faithful ρ-sets of S_n are small relative to the order of the group"; "we empirically observe that groups with larger ρ-sets relative to group order are more prone to failure mode (ρ-bad)"; non-vacuous bounds obtained for S4 and A5; "our per-neuron observations (B.2 1-3) hold for all finite groups we examine." K.2 — Frobenius-Schur indicator; complex irreps realified via Eq. 15; "the A, B, C matrices of Observation B.2(1) are approximately rank one when ρ is real, rank two when ρ is complex, and rank four when ρ is quaternionic … we speculate that the neural network uses the same ρ-sets algorithm as in the real case, but over C, and then takes the real part." No dihedral, affine, or Frobenius-group experiments are reported (0 hits for "dihedral", "affine" in the text).

**Verification:** compact proofs of accuracy lower bounds; "V_Coset failed to yield non-vacuous accuracy bounds" whereas the ρ-set verifier does (UNVERIFIED wording, from the HTML summary; the claim itself is in the paper).

**Bottom line for you:** Wu et al. own "irrep-sparse ∧ coset-constant ⇔ orbit of an H-fixed vector" and the min-ρ-set-size explanation of irrep frequency. They do not own: non-trivial characters, monomiality, M-groups, the induced *basis* (as opposed to one fixed vector), or any statement of the form "for these groups the two descriptions coincide."

## Section 4 — Which irreps get learned, and the 6d irrep of S5

**Empirical record.**
- Chughtai 2023, Fig. 7 (ReLU MLP, 50 runs on S5) — VERIFIED: sign and standard most common; standard⊗sign, 5d_a, 5d_b "approximately equally and less often"; "6d is never learned." Fig. 8: sign learned early, standard later. §6: dimension is *not* a good complexity measure ("standard sign offers better performance for fixed weight norm" yet is learned less).
- Wu 2025, App. H — VERIFIED: learning frequency ordering = ordering by **minimum ρ-set size** (sizes 1, 2, 5, 10, 6, 12, 20 for trivial, sign, std, sgn·std, 5d-0, 5d-1, 6d), because a complete ρ-set circuit needs |B|² neurons. The 6d irrep's minimal ρ-set is 20 (stabilizer Z/6Z or S3) → 400 neurons — far above m = 128. This is currently the only published *explanation* of 6d avoidance. [INFERENCE] Note this predicts 5d-0 (F20-stabilizer, size 6) should be learned about as often as standard (size 5), which is not what Chughtai's Fig. 7 shows (5d's are "less often"); Wu et al. do not comment on this discrepancy.
- Morwani 2024, Fig. 6(b) (quadratic activation, L_{2,3} regularization, S5) — VERIFIED: neurons distributed across **all** non-trivial irreps including 6d, "Higher dimensional representations have more neurons as given by our construction."

**Theory.**
- Morwani 2024, Theorem (App. I, VERIFIED): "Consider a single hidden layer neural network of width m with quadratic activation trained on learning group composition for G with real irreducible representations. Provided m ≥ 2Σ_{n=2}^K d_{R_n}³ and Σ_{n=2}^K d_{R_n}^{1.5} χ_{R_n}(C) < 0 for every non-trivial conjugacy class C, the L_{2,3} maximum margin is γ* = (2/3)·√(3|G|)·1/Σ_{n=2}^K d_{R_n}^{2.5}. Any network achieving this margin satisfies … 1. For every neuron, there exists a non-trivial representation such that the input and output weight vectors are spanned only by that representation. 2. There exists at least one neuron spanned by each representation (except for the trivial representation)." Main text (VERIFIED): "The condition that Σ d^{1.5} χ(C) < 0 for every non-trivial conjugacy class C holds for the symmetric group S_k up until k = 5." For general groups (App. I.2, VERIFIED): "only a subset of representations are present in the network; moreover, only a subset of conjugacy classes are present on the margin." Restricted to real irreps; no Frobenius–Schur discussion. **So max-margin theory predicts the 6d irrep *is* used (with weight ∝ some power of d), contradicting the ReLU-MLP observation** — the 6d avoidance is activation/architecture dependent.
- Marchetti 2026 (Sequential Group Composition), Theorem 4.1: utility ∝ ‖x̂[ρ]‖_op^{k+1}/(C_ρ n_ρ)^{(k−1)/2}; low-dimensional and *real* (C_ρ = 1) irreps favoured; bias grows with sequence length k; for k = 2 (binary composition) and one-hot inputs the dimension bias is weakest.
- He 2026: single-irrep convergence per neuron, no selection rule by dimension.
- Shutman 2025: rank bounds depend on irrep dimension and type (I/II/III); implicitly favours real irreps and small d (Strassen rank m_d).
- Wu 2025 K.2: complex/quaternionic irreps give rank-2/rank-4 A, B, C matrices — a concrete type-dependent signature, but no selection rule.

**Not found:** any work explaining irrep selection via Frobenius–Schur indicator *as a preference*, via monomiality, via "efficiency" of an induced basis, or via the number of subgroups with fixed vectors. Wu's ρ-set-size criterion is the only structural explanation; everything else is dimension/Fourier-power/type.

## Section 5 — Aff(Z_n), Frobenius groups, the action task, Ramanujan sums / composite moduli

**Aff(Z_n) / affine groups / semidirect products as *training* groups: nothing.** Zero arXiv-API hits for "affine group"/"Frobenius group"/"semidirect product" × grokking/mech-interp/group composition. Dihedral groups D_n = Z_n ⋊ Z_2 are the only semidirect products anyone has trained on (Chughtai D59, D61; Kvinge D30–D240; Marchetti D_n; McCracken 2025b UNVERIFIED). F20 = Aff(F_5) appears only as a *subgroup of S5*: Stander §6.1 (one neuron constant on F20 cosets; footnote identifying F20 with x ↦ ax + b over F5) and Wu Table 2/3 (F5 is the stabilizer of the minimal ρ-set of 5d-0, size 6).

**The action task (predict a·x + b, group acting on a set): nothing in mech-interp.** Closest: Petschack et al. (words in transpositions applied to a permutation, transformers, no circuit analysis); Marchetti et al. 2026 (k-fold products; regular representation only); Stojanović et al. (affine cipher, no interpretability). "Learning group actions" papers found are about latent-image manifolds (Hossain et al., UNVERIFIED), not finite-group circuits.

**Composite modulus / divisor / gcd structure.**
- McCracken 2025a (2505.18266): frequencies f with gcd(f, n) > 1 give neurons exactly constant on cosets of the subgroup of index n/gcd(f,n); "all architectures present a preference for learning precise cosets for n = 66"; general theory via "approximate cosets" {c − k₁d, …, c + k₂d} when gcd(f, n) = 1. This is the coset/divisor story for Z_n.
- McCracken 2026 (TAG-DS): (Z_m)^n, "Each divisor p_i^{k_i} marks out a coarser cycle: it partitions the m points into cosets"; image of an additive functional is gcd(m, αd₁, βd₂)Z_m.
- Chen 2026 (2607.07066): multiplicative monoid Z_n stratified by gcd(a, n); local groups (Z/(n/d)Z)^×; local characters.
- Doshi 2024, Nanda 2023, Zhong 2023, Gromov 2023: prime moduli only.
- **Ramanujan sums c_q(n): zero hits** in any ML/interp paper (arXiv API and web search). The Ramanujan-sum basis (sum of primitive q-th roots of unity, constant on gcd-classes) has not been proposed as a feature basis for composite-modulus networks. [INFERENCE] It is the natural real, integer-valued, gcd-block basis that sits between "single Fourier mode" (Nanda/Gromov) and "coset indicator" (McCracken), so this is open.

## Section 6 — Gaps (what nobody appears to have done)

1. **Non-trivial linear characters.** All existing coset↔irrep reasoning (Stander Lemma G.4, Wu Lemma G.6/Table 2–3) uses Ind_H^G 1 / H-fixed vectors only. Induction from a non-trivial χ, monomial irreps, and χ-twisted coset constancy are absent. Wu et al. even flag the trivial-summand problem as unexplained.
2. **M-groups as a class.** No paper defines or uses a group-class-level condition (monomial, supersolvable, nilpotent) to predict when coset and irrep descriptions coincide. The only group-class remark is Wu K.1 (S_n has small faithful ρ-sets; groups with large ρ-sets relative to |G| fail more).
3. **Per-irrep tests on S5 (monomial 6 vs non-monomial 4, 4′, 5, 5′).** Nobody has trained S5 networks that use the 6d irrep and looked at their coset structure. Note Warning A/B in Section 1 before designing the test.
4. **Aff(Z_n), Frobenius groups, other supersolvable non-dihedral groups (e.g. Z_p ⋊ Z_q, generalized quaternion, M5(2) except Shutman's tensor analysis).** No trained-network circuit analysis exists.
5. **The action task G × X → X.** Unstudied; it is the natural setting where induced representations Ind_{Stab(x)}^G appear directly (the permutation module C[X]).
6. **Ramanujan-sum / gcd-block bases for composite n.** Unstudied; McCracken's aCRT and Chen's J-classes are the nearest neighbours.
7. **Dihedral groups as the cleanest M-group test.** Chughtai reports Fourier-type 2d irreps on D59/D61; McCracken 2025b (UNVERIFIED) reports cosets on dihedral groups. Nobody has stated that these are the same structure (ρ_k = Ind_{Z_n}^{D_n} χ_k). [INFERENCE] For k coprime to n, ker χ_k is trivial, so the "twisted coset" reading gives no coset constancy — the two bases (Fourier-on-rotations vs. reflection-axis orbit) are genuinely different bases of the same irrep, which makes D_n a good place to test *which basis the network actually uses* rather than assuming the dispute dissolves.
8. **Discrepancy between Wu's ρ-set-size ordering and Chughtai's Fig. 7 for the 5d irreps** (5d-0 has ρ-set size 6 yet is learned less than standard): unexplained; Morwani's quadratic nets use all irreps, ReLU nets do not — no paper compares activation functions on irrep selection.
9. **Reviewer discussions** (OpenReview) for Wu and Stander are unread (blocked) — worth checking manually for any mention of induced representations before submission.
