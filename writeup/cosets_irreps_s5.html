0Summary

Small neural networks trained to multiply elements of the symmetric group S₅ have been reverse-engineered twice with different conclusions: Chughtai et al. (2023) say the hidden neurons compute irreducible representations (irreps) of the group; Stander et al. (2024) say the neurons are constant on cosets of subgroups. Our project's thesis is that these are two coordinate systems on one construction — the induced representation — and that the disagreement should vanish on groups whose irreps are all monomial (induced from one-dimensional characters), such as the affine groups Aff(ℤₙ).

This week we (a) ran a literature check on that thesis and (b) built and validated the infrastructure to test it on Chughtai's released S₅ models. The literature check found that the trivial-character half of the thesis is already published — Wu, Jaburi, Drori & Gross (ICLR 2025) prove that a neuron is both "irrep-sparse" and "coset-concentrated" exactly when it is the orbit of a subgroup-fixed vector — but that nobody has touched non-trivial characters, monomiality, M-groups, Aff(ℤₙ), or the "action" task. Wu et al. even name the gap we would fill. Two of our specific predictions needed correcting, and are corrected below.

The infrastructure is done: the 120 group elements are pinned to the exact indexing the models were trained with (verified by 100.00% accuracy on a table we rebuilt from scratch), every representation-theoretic claim we rely on is now checked by a program rather than by hand, and we reproduced Chughtai's own published per-irrep numbers to three decimals. Along the way, two facts fell out without training anything: the 6-dimensional irrep — the one irrep of S₅ with a genuinely "coset-shaped" basis — carries essentially zero hidden-layer energy in all 60 released models that generalize and 10–26% in every model that merely memorized; and Morwani et al.'s margin-maximization theorem predicts the opposite (that 6d is used), so its absence is a property of the ReLU/cross-entropy training regime, not of the task.

The S₅ probe was then pre-registered and run (P1 passed; P2 failed at its threshold because memorizers are not a null; P3 passed), and the plan in §7 was executed as an Aff(ℤₙ) sweep whose results are in §8. The network builds plain-coset, rank-one circuits on the affine groups too, even where a slack-free Fourier basis exists. What varies by seed is which exact realization it picks.

1The problem

1.1 The task and the network

Take a finite group G — here S₅, the 120 permutations of five objects — and train a network to compute the product. The input is a pair of elements (x, y), each given as a one-hot vector of length 120; the output is a probability distribution over the 120 possible products; the training set is a random 40% of the 14,400 pairs. Nothing about the group is told to the network. When it eventually reaches 100% on the held-out 60%, it has discovered some internal encoding of the group's structure, and the interpretability question is: which one?

The architecture in all three papers is the same one-hidden-layer MLP. Two embedding matrices Wx, Wy (120 × 256) look up the two inputs; the two embeddings are concatenated and multiplied by W (512 × 128); a ReLU is applied; and an unembedding WU (128 × 120) produces logits. Because W acts on a concatenation, the pre-activation of neuron j splits into two pieces that depend on one input each:

The object we study

prej(x, y) = ujx(x) + ujy(y),   hiddenj = ReLU(prej),

where ujx = (Wx W[:256, j]) is a list of 120 numbers — one per group element — i.e. a function on the group. Everything below is about the shape of these functions.

Training runs for 250,000 epochs with AdamW (weight decay 1). The network first memorizes the training set, then, tens of thousands of epochs later, abruptly generalizes — "grokking". Chughtai et al. released the final weights of 50 seeds at this configuration, plus a sweep over narrower hidden layers and four one-layer transformers; those 84 models are our raw material (§5.1).

1.2 The two published readings

Chughtai, Chan & Nanda (ICML 2023) proposed the "Group Composition via Representations" (GCR) algorithm: the embeddings map x and y to matrices ρ(x), ρ(y) of some irrep ρ; the network effectively computes the trace tr(ρ(x)ρ(y)ρ(z)−1), which is maximized at z = xy. Their evidence is basis-free: the fraction of hidden-layer energy that lies in each irrep's block (§2.2), and the cosine similarity of the logits to the trace formula. They found that on S₅ the network uses the 4-dimensional "standard" irrep most often, the sign irrep always, the two 5-dimensional irreps sometimes, and the 6-dimensional irrep never.

Stander, Yu, Fan & Biderman (ICML 2024) retrained the same setup and looked at the pre-activations ujx directly. They found that almost every neuron takes only a handful of distinct values, and that the elements sharing a value form the cosets of some subgroup H ⊂ S₅ — the alternating group A₅, the point-stabilizers S₄, the Frobenius group F₂₀, the dihedral group D₁₀. They describe the mechanism as "coset circuits" and write: "We were not able to find any evidence that the linear layer implements matrix multiplication."

The two papers measure different things (energy in an irrep block vs. constancy on a partition) and reach conclusions that sound contradictory. The question this project asks is whether they are.

2The representation theory you need

Everything in this section is standard; the point is to fix the notation and the five facts the rest of the writeup leans on.

2.1 Irreps and characters of S₅

A representation of G is a homomorphism ρ: G → GL(d) — a way of writing each group element as a d × d matrix so that ρ(xy) = ρ(x)ρ(y). It is irreducible if no proper subspace is preserved by all the ρ(g). S₅ has exactly seven irreps, one for each conjugacy class (equivalently, one for each partition of 5), with dimensions 1, 1, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6. The character χρ(g) = tr ρ(g) is a function on G that is constant on conjugacy classes and determines ρ up to change of basis. The character table (rows: irreps in Chughtai's naming; columns: classes by cycle type, with class sizes) is:

2.2 Functions on the group split into blocks

A function f: G → ℝ is a vector in ℝ¹²⁰. The matrix entries ρ(g)ij of an irrep, viewed as functions of g, span a subspace Vρ of dimension dρ², and these subspaces are mutually orthogonal and fill the space: 1 + 1 + 16 + 16 + 25 + 25 + 36 = 120. So every function on G decomposes uniquely into pieces living in the seven blocks — this is Fourier analysis on a non-abelian group, and Vρ is called the ρ-isotypic component. The projection onto Vρ needs only the character:

(Pρ f)(g) = (dρ/|G|) Σh χρ(h) f(h−1g).

Chughtai's "percent of hidden energy in irrep ρ" is exactly ‖Pρ f‖² / ‖f‖² for the relevant functions, which is why it is basis-independent — and why it cannot, by itself, distinguish a "coset neuron" from a generic vector in the same block.

2.3 Subgroups, cosets, coset-constant functions

For a subgroup H ⊂ G, the left cosets gH partition G into |G|/|H| blocks of size |H| (right cosets Hg give a different partition unless H is normal). A function is H-coset-constant if it takes one value per block. Which irreps can such a function contain? The answer is a classical one: the coset-constant functions form the permutation representation of G on G/H, and its irreducible constituents are given by the induced representation IndHG(1) of the trivial character. For example IndS₄(1) = trivial ⊕ standard, so an S₄-coset-constant function has energy only in those two blocks (Figure 1, row 1). Note the slack: the constituent we care about (standard, 4-dim) comes packaged with a trivial component we do not (the 5 coset values have 4 degrees of freedom once the mean is removed). §5.4 tabulates this for all 19 conjugacy classes of subgroups of S₅.

2.4 Induced representations and Frobenius reciprocity

More generally, start with a one-dimensional character λ: H → ℂ× and build IndHG(λ): a representation of dimension |G/H| whose basis vectors are indexed by cosets and on which each ρ(g) acts as a permutation matrix with entries multiplied by values of λ — a monomial matrix. Concretely, IndH(λ) is realized by the functions f on G with f(gh) = λ(h)−1 f(g) for h ∈ H: "constant on cosets up to a phase." The dictionary between this and the isotypic blocks is Frobenius reciprocity,

⟨IndH(λ), ρ⟩ = ⟨λ, ResH(ρ)⟩,

which says: ρ appears in IndH(λ) exactly as many times as ρ, restricted to H, contains a vector b with ρ(h)b = λ(h)b. For λ = 1 this is "ρ has an H-fixed vector". This one line is the whole reconciliation: a neuron of the form g ↦ aᵀρ(g)b with b fixed by H is simultaneously an irrep neuron (it lies in Vρ) and a coset neuron (it is constant on cosets of H).

2.5 Monomial irreps and the dissolution idea

An irrep is monomial if it equals IndH(λ) for some H and some one-dimensional λ — i.e. it has a basis in which every ρ(g) is a monomial matrix, so its coordinates are coset coordinates (with phases). A group all of whose irreps are monomial is an M-group; all supersolvable groups are M-groups, which covers ℤₙ, dihedral groups, and Aff(ℤₙ). On such a group an "irrep circuit" and a "coset circuit" are literally the same object in two bases, with no slack. The symmetric group S₅ is not an M-group (Taketa's theorem: M-groups are solvable), which is why the dispute could arise there at all. For S₅, our exact computations give:

  • trivial, sign: monomial (1-dimensional);
  • 6d = IndF₂₀(λ₁) = IndF₂₀(λ₃), where λ₁, λ₃ are the two order-4 characters of F₂₀ ≅ C₅ ⋊ C₄ — monomial;
  • standard, standard′, 5a, 5b: not monomial (S₅ has no subgroup of index 4; its index-5 subgroups are the S₄'s, whose two linear characters give trivial ⊕ standard and sign ⊕ standard′, not irreps; the index-6 subgroup F₂₀ gives trivial ⊕ 5a, sign ⊕ 5b, and 6d).

Every non-monomial irrep of S₅ is nonetheless "monomial up to one linear constituent": IndH(λ) = ρ ⊕ (one 1-dim irrep). We call that one extra constituent the slack. All four non-monomial irreps of S₅ have slack exactly 1, so S₅ offers no gradation in slack; S₆ and larger do.

3The hypothesis going in

As of the start of this week the project's claims were:

  1. Dissolution. Stander's cosets and Chughtai's irreps are two bases of the same induced-representation construction wherever the irrep is monomial; the dispute should dissolve, not resolve, on M-groups. The clean testbed is the family Aff(ℤₙ) = ℤₙ ⋊ ℤₙ×, whose higher irreps are all induced from characters of the translation subgroup.
  2. Per-irrep split on S₅. Monomial irreps {trivial, sign, 6d} should show coset organization; non-monomial {standard, standard′, 5a, 5b} should not.
  3. The 6d signature. A 6d neuron should be constant on cosets of C₅ with a 4-periodic rotation across F₂₀/C₅ ≅ C₄ — a "clock" on the 5-cycle subgroup. (Plain F₂₀-coset-constant functions carry no 6d weight; Figure 1.)
  4. The puzzle. Chughtai report that 6d is never learned and Stander report no C₅ circuits: the one higher irrep with a genuine coset coordinate system is the one networks avoid.

Claims 2 and 3 are the ones the literature review forced us to revise (§4.6).

4Literature review

A background agent swept arXiv, Semantic Scholar and the relevant PDFs for anything connecting induced representations, monomiality, or non-trivial characters to network features; the full annotated report is lit_review.md. Here is what matters.

4.1 Wu, Jaburi, Drori & Gross (ICLR 2025 spotlight)

"Towards a Unified and Verified Understanding of Group-Operation Networks" (arXiv:2410.07476) trains 100 one-layer S₅ models (width 128, 40% train, Adam) and sets out explicitly to unify the two prior accounts. Their key definitions and result:

Wu et al., Definition 6.3 and Lemma G.6 (verified from the PDF)

A function f: G → ℝ is a projected ρ-set if f(g) = aᵀρ(g)b for some irrep ρ and vectors a, b, where b has a non-trivial stabilizer H = {h : ρ(h)b = b}. Lemma G.6: f is both irrep-sparse (Chughtai's condition) and coset-concentrated (Stander's condition) if and only if f is a projected ρ-set. The proof builds the permutation representation on G/H — in their words, "ρ̃ is induced by the trivial representation on H."

That is the trivial-character instance of §2.4, published. They also observe empirically that each neuron's three coefficient matrices are rank one and that the b-vectors of all neurons on a given irrep form finite G-orbits ("ρ-sets"), and they explain the frequency with which each irrep is learned by the size of its smallest ρ-set — the smallest index of a subgroup with a fixed vector — rather than by dimension: standard needs an orbit of size 5 (stabilizer S₄), standard′ needs 10 (A₄), 5a needs 6 (F₂₀), 5b needs 10 (S₃×S₂), 6d needs 20 (S₃ or C₆). A complete circuit needs |orbit|² neurons, so 6d would need 400 — more than the layer has. (Their Table 2 prints 12 for 5b, but their own Table 3 gives the D₁₂ ≅ S₃×S₂ orbit size 10, which is correct.)

Crucially, they name what the trivial-character picture leaves open (their §6.3): the permutation representation on G/H "is never itself an irrep; it always contains the trivial irrep… Thus, it does not explain why the models' neurons are supported purely on single irreps." Induction from a non-trivial character is precisely the construction that produces an irrep with no trivial summand. The words "monomial", "M-group", "linear character", "twist" do not occur in the paper.

4.2 Stander et al., revisited

Re-reading Stander with the reciprocity lens: their Appendix G.2 already uses Frobenius reciprocity for the trivial character, and their Table 5 tabulates, for every conjugacy class of subgroups of S₅, how the Fourier energy of the (centered) coset indicator splits across irreps — S₄ → 100% standard, F₂₀ → 100% 5a, A₅ → 100% sign, D₁₀ → 45.5% / 45.5% on 5a and 5b, S₃×S₂ → 55.6% standard / 44.4% 5b. Our dictionary (§5.4) reproduces this row for row. Two further things from their appendix matter for us. Their coset statistic is CH(f) = (within-coset variance)/(total variance), minimized over all 156 subgroups per neuron per side — we will use exactly 1 − CH, so the results are comparable. And they record a puzzle: coset neurons on subgroups like D₁₀, whose indicator spans two irreps, nonetheless "always do concentrate one irrep… We do not have an explanation for why," adding "We do not think that there is in fact a connection between what the circuit is doing [and] the irrep." The induced-module picture — a neuron is a vector in Im(QH) ∩ Vρ, the ρ-part of the induced module — is that explanation, with the single-irrep part supplied by margin maximization (§4.3).

4.3 Morwani, Edelman, Oncescu, Zhang & Barak (ICLR 2024)

"Feature emergence via margin maximization" proves, for one-hidden-layer networks with quadratic activation and L2,3 regularization on group composition, that any maximum-margin network has every neuron supported on a single non-trivial irrep and has at least one neuron on every non-trivial irrep. The theorem's hypothesis Σ dρ1.5 χρ(C) < 0 holds for Sk up to k = 5, and their own S₅ experiment shows neurons on the 6d irrep. So the "6d is never learned" fact of §6.1 is not a consequence of margin maximization as such; it is a property of the ReLU / cross-entropy / weight-decay regime. This turns claim 4 of §3 from a puzzle into a lever (§7.1).

4.4 Other relevant work

  • Marchetti et al. (2026), sequential group composition: a utility rule favouring low-dimensional and real irreps. He (2026): proof of single-irrep convergence per neuron. Neither mentions cosets or induction.
  • McCracken et al. (2025/2026): composite-modulus modular arithmetic shows approximate-CRT and "precise coset" structure when gcd(f, n) > 1 — the closest thing to our divisor-lattice predictions for Aff(ℤₙ), on the additive side only. Chen et al. (2026): gcd-stratified structure for modular multiplication.
  • Not obtained: McCracken, Wei, Moisescu-Pareja, Wiltzer & Love, "The representations of deep neural networks trained on dihedral group multiplication" (NeurReps @ NeurIPS 2025). Dihedral groups are M-groups; a follow-up reports coset structure there. This is the nearest existing test of the dissolution claim and must be read before we write anything.
  • No paper trains on Aff(ℤₙ), a Frobenius group, or the action task (predict a·x + b rather than a composition); no paper mentions Ramanujan sums.

4.6 Two corrections to the hypothesis

Correction A — the per-irrep split (claim 2) is wrong as stated

"Non-monomial irreps should not show coset organization" is refuted by the record: Stander's headline S₅ circuit is built from S₄-coset neurons, and those live in the standard irrep, which is not monomial. Wu et al. exhibit a coset-constant vector for every S₅ irrep. What monomiality changes is not whether coset structure exists but its slack: for a monomial irrep there is an (H, λ) with IndH(λ) = ρ exactly, so the |G/H| = dρ coset values are unconstrained irrep coordinates; for a slack-1 irrep the |G/H| = dρ + 1 coset values satisfy one linear constraint (Stander's own S₄ neuron takes values 4, 2, 0, −2, −4, summing to zero). On S₅ every learned irrep has slack 1, so the graded prediction can only be tested across a family of groups.

Correction B — the C₅ "clock" (claim 3) is not a discriminating signature

The C₅-fixed subspace of the 6d irrep is 2-dimensional (6d appears twice in IndC₅(1); Figure 1, last row) and F₂₀/C₅ ≅ C₄ acts on it by λ₁ ⊕ λ̄₁. So any C₅-fixed vector in 6d rotates by 90° under a generator of F₂₀/C₅: the "twist" is a property of restricting 6d to F₂₀, and a Wu-style orbit with stabilizer C₅ has it automatically. The question that does discriminate is which orbit a 6d circuit would use: Wu's minimum-size rule says the S₃/C₆ orbit (size 20, plain cosets, lots of slack); an induction-based account says the F₂₀-structured C₅ orbit (size 24, the realification of IndF₂₀(λ₁), no slack). This is testable only in a regime that actually learns 6d — Morwani's (§4.3).

5.2 Conventions, validated rather than assumed

Two things could have silently corrupted every result. First, the group elements are indexed by the order in which SymPy 1.11.1 happened to enumerate S₅; SymPy 1.14 no longer populates that list the same way. We installed 1.11.1 in a throwaway environment, dumped the ordering to data/s5_elements_sympy1111.json, and rebuilt the 120 × 120 multiplication table from it. Second, "x·y" could mean apply-x-then-y or the reverse. The check that settles both at once: a generalizing model scores 100.00% on our rebuilt table under apply-x-then-y and 5.83% under the reverse — the latter being exactly the fraction of commuting pairs in S₅. Stander compose right-to-left, so their (σleft, σright) is Chughtai's (y, x); we test both coset sides regardless.

5.3 The probe package and what its self-tests prove

s5_probe/s5probe/ is a small, dependency-light library; tests/selftest.py runs in a few seconds and currently passes 50 checks. What they establish:

  • Group (group.py): the table is associative with correct inverses; the seven conjugacy classes have sizes 1, 10, 15, 20, 20, 30, 24; named subgroups A₅, S₄, F₂₀, D₁₀, C₅, S₃×S₂, S₃, C₄ are closed with the right orders and numbers of conjugates (F₂₀: 6, S₄: 5, C₅: 6).
  • Characters (chars.py): the character table's rows are orthonormal; the seven isotypic projectors are idempotent, symmetric, have ranks dρ², and sum to the identity.
  • Induced characters: IndA₅(1) = 1 ⊕ sgn; IndS₄(1) = 1 ⊕ std; IndS₄(sgn) = sgn ⊕ std′; IndF₂₀(λ₀) = 1 ⊕ 5a; IndF₂₀(λ₂) = sgn ⊕ 5b; IndF₂₀(λ₁) = IndF₂₀(λ₃) = 6d; IndC₅(1) = 1 ⊕ sgn ⊕ 5a ⊕ 5b ⊕ 2·6d. The characters λk of F₂₀ are built from the conjugation action on C₅ and checked to be homomorphisms under the model's own operation.
  • Twisted-coset projectors: the projector onto F₂₀-coset-constant functions has rank 6 and is annihilated by the 6d projector; the λ₁-twisted projector has rank 6 and lies entirely inside 6d; its image is constant on C₅-cosets. (Both sides.) This is Figure 1, proved.
  • Pipeline (metrics.py): our basis-free re-implementation of Chughtai's per-irrep hidden-energy metric reproduces every number in their summary_metrics.json to within 1.3 × 10−3 on generalizing models — so the loader, indexing, projectors and centering are all consistent with theirs.

5.5 Null distributions — what "coset-organized" has to beat

6Findings before any new experiment

6.1 The 6d irrep's energy marks memorization

Chughtai's summary files record, for each run, the fraction of centered hidden-layer energy in each irrep block. Plotting that fraction for the 6d block against the run's test accuracy, across all 84 released S₅ models, gives Figure 2.

Two consequences. There is no generalizing 6d substrate in the released weights, so the twisted-orbit test of Correction B cannot be run on them; it needs a model trained to use 6d. And the statement "6d is never learned" sharpens to something with teeth: 6d energy in a generalizing model is zero, and 6d energy in the hidden layer is a marker of failure to generalize.

6.2 What generalizing models actually use

Across the 50 mainline seeds the standard irrep is present in 49 (in 21 of them it carries at least 80% of hidden energy on its own), sign in 49 (at 1–8%), standard′ in 17, 5a in 18, 5b in 10, 6d in none. So the per-irrep test of §3 claim 2 reduces, on this substrate, to: sign (monomial, trivially coset-constant on A₅ — uninformative), and four slack-1 irreps.

6.3 The Morwani contrast

Morwani's theorem covers S₅ and requires every non-trivial irrep, 6d included, to be present in a max-margin quadratic network; their own S₅ figure shows 6d neurons. ReLU networks trained with cross-entropy and weight decay never use it. So the selection of irreps is decided by the training regime, and a network that does learn 6d is obtainable by switching to Morwani's. That is the concrete content of "plan option B theoretically": the discriminating 6d experiment is which orbit (S₃, size 20 vs. F₂₀-structured C₅, size 24) a Morwani-regime network chooses, and it would separate Wu's minimum-orbit rule from an induction-based account.

7The plan from here

7.1 Sharpened predictions

  • Slack, not monomial-vs-not. On any group, a coset-aligned neuron on an index-k subgroup carrying irrep ρ has k coset values with k − dρ linear constraints. On M-groups that number is 0 for a suitable (H, λ); on S₅ it is 1 for every learned irrep; on S₆+ it varies. The family sweep is where this becomes a test.
  • Which orbit for 6d (Correction B), in Morwani's regime.
  • Aff(ℤₙ): for composite n the translation subgroup's divisor lattice turns on, and every irrep is exactly Ind of a character of ℤₙ — the case with no slack anywhere, where Chughtai's and Stander's descriptions must coincide neuron by neuron.

7.3 The paper fork

Two papers were on the table. The composition / dissolution paper is now "extend Wu from trivial to non-trivial characters; show the dispute dissolves on M-groups; sweep the Aff(ℤₙ) family" — a real increment on a spotlight paper, with a possible partial pre-emption in the unread dihedral workshop paper. The action-side paper (train on a·x + b, i.e. the permutation representation of Aff(ℤₙ) with its Ramanujan-sum characters and divisor blocks) sits in territory with no prior work at all. The S₅ probe was chosen to decide between them; the literature result tilts toward the action side without settling it.

8The affine family: results

Everything below was run against results/preregistration_affine_v1.md, frozen on 2026-09-15 before any affine model was trained; the amendments and dev-phase observations recorded there are summarized where they matter. Thirty-five models were trained on this laptop (Chughtai's architecture, two recipes, five seeds per cell); thirty-one reached test accuracy ≥ 0.99 and enter the analysis. The four that did not are the three Aff(ℤ₁₃) runs — that group never generalizes at width 128 under the Adam recipe, even at 100k epochs — and one Aff(ℤ₁₁) seed at 25k epochs, which the same seed at 100k epochs did solve: Aff(ℤ₁₁) sits at a grokking threshold, Aff(ℤ₁₃) beyond it. The composites grok by epoch 300–800.

8.1 Two things the mathematics decided before the network did

Factoring. By the Chinese remainder theorem Aff(ℤ₁₅) ≅ S₃ × F₂₀ and Aff(ℤ₂₁) ≅ S₃ × Aff(F₇). The 8- and 12-dimensional "top" irreps of those groups are tensor products of the factors' irreps, and every one of the fifteen models on them leaves that block empty: the network solves the two factors separately and never builds the tensor. So the only composite modulus without an escape hatch is 16, and the pre-registered slack comparison rests on n = 11 versus n = 16.

Siblings. Each block ρ_d has character-twisted siblings ρ_d ⊗ χ that are equally good for the task — the S₅ standard/standard′ choice, made at random: in about 40% of cells the network used a sibling of the block the pre-registration named (five of fifteen models on Aff(ℤ₁₅), three of five on Aff(ℤ₁₆)'s d = 8 block, two of five on Aff(ℤ₂₁)'s d = 7). The frozen verdict for those is 'empty'; the descriptive layer scores siblings with the plain-with-character family (M_x, λ).

8.3 What the residue contains

Two descriptive tools were added after dev, before confirm analysis, and both are recorded in the pre-registration: Wu et al.'s rank test — is the neuron's coefficient matrix in the block rank one, i.e. a projected ρ-set of some subgroup — and a uniform Stander-style sweep over every subgroup class of index ≤ 16 with every linear character, against Monte-Carlo nulls (informative for blocks of dimension ≥ 6; vacuous for dimension 4, and read accordingly).

  • Rank one is nearly universal. Every block-dominant neuron in every model is rank one (r1 ≥ 0.99 for 100% of energy) except the top block of one Aff(ℤ₁₆) seed, where 74% of the energy is rank two. Wu's form holds on M-groups essentially without exception.
  • Aff(ℤ₁₁): one answer. 99–100% of top-block energy in all six models is constant on point-stabilizer cosets with the trivial character (null p99 0.29). The prime case is Stander's S₄ circuit transplanted.
  • Aff(ℤ₂₁), d = 7: the S₅ 'A4' phenomenon. Energy splits between the mod-7 point stabilizer and its index-2 subgroup — neurons mixing the plain and the character-twisted realization, exactly what the impure S₄-type columns were on S₅.
  • Aff(ℤ₁₆): the menu. Seed 1: point stabilizers (98%). Seed 5: a complement of the translations that is not conjugate to any point stabilizer, with a character (54%) — Aff(ℤ₁₆) has such complements because ℤ₁₆ has 2-torsion. Seed 3: a mixture of classes. Seed 4: rank one, but the vector is supported on a Klein-four-group orbit of frequencies — a stabilizer strictly between the translation subgroup and the whole group, matching no linear-character family. Seed 2: rank two — two frequency assignments on the two cosets of T ⋊ V₄, induced from a four-dimensional irrep of an index-2 subgroup. The sibling d = 8 block (seeds 3–5) is exactly constant (α = 1.00) on cosets of an order-16 subgroup that contains no point stabilizer at all; every stabilizer family scores 0 there.
  • Sides. The x-input reads right cosets (a function of x⁻¹(0)), the y-input left cosets (a function of y(0)), in every grokked model, with the two halves carrying equal energy to within 0.5% — the same translation of Stander's construction that held on S₅.

8.4 Reading

On the affine family the network almost always builds rank-one projected ρ-sets whose stabilizer is a point stabilizer, which is Stander's coset organization written in Wu's form, even though an exact Fourier basis with zero slack is available in every block. From seed to seed what varies is which exact realization is used: which sibling irrep, which subgroup class, and, on the one composite group without factoring, occasionally a structure that is not a coset structure at all. The pre-registered dichotomy was answered in favour of plain cosets. The structure worth writing about is in the cases the dichotomy did not name. The dissolution thesis survives in a weaker form than the one we started with: every organization observed is an exact induced-module basis of the same irrep, but induction from a linear character is not the whole menu, and monomiality does not say which item the network will use.

A.3 Conventions and glossary

x·y
apply x, then y (SymPy's convention; the models' target). Chughtai's x is Stander's right input.
g·H, H·g
"left" and "right" cosets with respect to that operation; every statistic is computed for both sides.
Vρ, Pρ
isotypic block of irrep ρ (dimension dρ²) and the projector onto it
QH, QHλ
projector onto H-coset-constant functions; onto λ-twisted ones (f(gh) = λ̄(h) f(g))
IndH(λ)
representation induced from the character λ of H; its irreducible constituents are the irreps that twisted-coset functions can carry
slack
number of extra 1-dim constituents in the cheapest IndH(λ) containing ρ; 0 ⇔ ρ monomial
αH, CH
coset-alignment score and Stander's coset-concentration; α = 1 − C
projected ρ-set
Wu et al.: a neuron g ↦ aᵀρ(g)b with b having a non-trivial stabilizer
grokking
delayed generalization: memorize first, generalize much later
